A connected approach

Healing is more than a single moment.

A safe place to begin matters. So does having the support to continue.

Recovery is a process of rebuilding well-being, strengthening relationships, and discovering new possibilities. There is no single timeline, and each person’s needs and goals are different.

Denver Rescue Mission's five core strategies work together. Recovery may happen alongside access to shelter, personal development, and help moving toward stable housing.

The recovery strategy

The human side of recovery

Progress deserves room to grow.

Recovery may involve rebuilding trust, establishing routines, navigating difficult emotions, or learning how to approach life differently. None of those changes happens in isolation.

Staff, volunteers, peers, and community partners can offer support and encouragement along the way. What progress looks like will vary by individual, but the goal is consistent: greater well-being and an opportunity for stability.

Questions & next steps

Understanding Recovery

What does Recovery mean in the five-strategy continuum?

Recovery describes work that supports healing and personal well-being. It may overlap with Dignity, Readiness, and Housing rather than functioning as a separate, one-size-fits-all step.

Does every person follow the same path?

No. Each person's circumstances are different. The five strategies explain how types of support connect; they are not a required order of participation.

Which programs connect with Recovery?

The New Life Program is one important example. Other services may intersect with recovery depending on needs and program offerings. Confirm specific eligibility and services through Denver Rescue Mission's current program information.

Where can someone ask about services?

Visit Denver Rescue Mission's Get Help page for current assistance information. This concept page does not establish eligibility or service availability.
