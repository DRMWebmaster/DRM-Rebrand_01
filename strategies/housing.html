Housing.

How the five strategies connect

Helping people reach housing stability

Housing means helping people overcome barriers, find stable housing, and work toward maintaining it. It is a cross-cutting outcome and set of supports—not a claim that the Mission currently owns or operates permanent housing as part of this strategy. Explore our programs →

What housing means

More than
four walls.

A home can be the beginning of greater stability—not simply the end of a difficult chapter.

When someone is experiencing homelessness, finding housing can mean navigating complicated systems, unexpected barriers, and a great deal of uncertainty. Stable housing is essential, but the path there is not the same for everyone.

Denver Rescue Mission’s Housing strategy focuses on helping people move toward safe, sustainable housing opportunities while recognizing that support, relationships, and practical preparation often matter along the way.

Prevention, Dignity, Recovery, and Readiness may all intersect with Housing. The five strategies are connected—not a checklist that every person must complete in order.

The work behind stability

Every path home
has moving parts.

Housing-focused support can involve several kinds of practical steps. These examples illustrate the strategy—not a promise that every service is available through every program.

01 / EXPLORE

Understand the barriers.

Identify challenges such as documentation, income, transportation, or finding appropriate housing options.

02 / CONNECT

Navigate opportunities.

Build a practical plan, understand next steps, and connect with available housing-related resources and community support.

03 / SUSTAIN

Strengthen stability.

Consider what ongoing relationships, routines, and other supports can help a household move forward.

REPLACE WITH APPROVED MISSION PHOTOHousing in real life

The next step is personal.

For one neighbor, the first task may be replacing an ID. For another, it may be exploring an apartment application, rebuilding income, or finding support during a transition.

Housing-focused work is most meaningful when it responds to a person's actual circumstances—not assumptions about what they should need.

  • A clear understanding of immediate obstacles
  • Connections to relevant housing opportunities and resources
  • Practical support that recognizes the need for ongoing stability
See How Programs Connect ↗︎ The larger goal

A home makes room
for possibility.

Stable housing can help create the conditions for stronger relationships, better health, employment, and a renewed sense of belonging. The path may be complex; the goal is worth pursuing.

Beyond an address

Housing and support
go hand in hand.

A set of keys can represent an important milestone. Long-term stability may also depend on people to call, a plan for difficult moments, and resources that make daily life manageable.

This is why the Housing strategy is connected to every other part of the Mission's approach—even when the right services look different from one neighbor to the next.

How strategies intersect

Different needs.
Connected support.

Housing can connect with immediate dignity and care, healing through recovery, readiness for new responsibilities, and prevention that helps people stay housed.

The continuum is not a mandatory progression. People may draw on more than one strategy at once.
