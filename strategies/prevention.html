A STRATEGY FOR LASTING STABILITY

How the five strategies connect

A strategy and a program

Prevention is a Mission-wide strategy: addressing risks before they become homelessness. The Prevention Program is a specific way Denver Rescue Mission puts that broader strategy into practice. Prevention also connects to dignity, readiness, recovery and housing. Explore our programs →

A connected approach

Stability is worth protecting.

When a neighbor faces a setback, the right support at the right moment can change what happens next.

For a family living paycheck to paycheck, a missed shift, an unexpected repair, or a sudden change in circumstances can threaten the stability they've worked hard to build.

Prevention is the work of recognizing that risk early, connecting people with practical help, and making room for a different outcome. It is one part of Denver Rescue Mission's wider continuum of support.

Act early

Address a difficult situation before it becomes a larger crisis.

See the whole person

Recognize that housing concerns often intersect with relationships, health, and everyday needs.

Strengthen stability

Support practical next steps that can help households regain their footing.

Understanding the challenge

A housing crisis rarely begins all at once.

The signs may be small at first. Early support can create more options before instability grows.

The moments before a crisis
  1. 01A sudden financial setback

    An unexpected expense, income change, or missed paycheck disrupts a household budget.

  2. 02Hard choices follow

    Essentials begin to compete with one another, creating pressure around rent, utilities, transportation, or groceries.

  3. 03Options become narrower

    Without timely support, a temporary disruption can become more difficult to resolve.

  4. 04Support changes the equation

    Practical resources, connection, and individualized guidance may help a neighbor stabilize.

The purpose of prevention

Help people stay where stability can grow.

Prevention is not simply about responding to an emergency. It's about preserving the progress people have already made and helping create the conditions for continued independence.

A single difficult season should not have to determine someone's future.

The five-strategy continuum

Prevention isn't a separate destination.

People's needs don't always fit into neat categories. Prevention connects with other strategies—from Dignity and Recovery to Readiness and Housing—as circumstances change.

Not everyone will move through all five strategies, and not everyone experiences them in the same order. Our continuum describes interconnected approaches, not a required sequence.
