Homelessness in Denver

Behind every statistic is a person, a family and a story. Understanding the challenges our neighbors face is an important step toward helping people find stability.

Understanding the need

The Faces and Families Behind Homelessness

Denver’s housing costs, substance-use challenges and other pressures leave many individuals and families uncertain about food, shelter and safety. The figures below reflect the statistics currently published on the Mission’s live Homelessness in Denver page.

10,774People experiencing homelessness in the Denver metro area
80%Seek shelter with organizations like Denver Rescue Mission to stay safe overnight
30%Are struggling families, many experiencing homelessness for the first time
34%Have experienced chronic homelessness

20% of the homeless population in Metro Denver is unsheltered, including people living outdoors, in cars or in other places not intended for habitation.

Figures reproduced from the Mission’s current page; reporting year is not identified there. Source: Metro Denver Homeless Initiative, Point-in-Time Count. Please check the source for newer data.
Why homelessness happens

Causes and Barriers

Homelessness is complex. These are some of the challenges that can make housing difficult to obtain or maintain.

01

Lack of affordable housing

Rapidly rising housing costs can put stable homes beyond the reach of people and families with low incomes.

02

Chronic health conditions

Illness, injury and limited access to healthcare may disrupt work, income and the ability to maintain housing.

03

Domestic violence

Leaving an unsafe home can mean losing housing and the support or resources needed to begin again.

04

Job loss

Unemployment, low wages and the cost of living can quickly put a household at risk of losing its housing.

05

Substance abuse

Without access to appropriate treatment and support, addiction can become a major barrier to stability.
